# Wave settings and balancing (TUNING)

Every number in this table is live in `Assets/Data/Echo/EchoTuning.asset`, i.e. `Assets/Scripts/Runtime/Data/EchoTuningProfile.cs`. None of them are hard-coded in C#, so changing the balance needs no compile and can be tested in the editor with Play. The character's motion is the one deliberate exception: those numbers are `[SerializeField]` on `PlayerVisual` in the scene and are listed under "The player's art and motion" at the end of this file. The profile is the balance brain — `OnValidate` enforces its invariants and `MovementAndHearingTests` asserts them — and a breathing rate is not balance.

The target these numbers have to hit is [GDD.md](../GDD.md) v3.0, whose tuning knob is **`ρ = enemy hearing radius / visible echo reach` at the same loudness**, starting at `0.8`. If `ρ` is too high, pinging is never worth it; if too low, there is no tension.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. `waveSpeed >= 1.6 * walkSpeed` — if the arc travels slower than 1.6 times the player's speed, the returning arc can never overtake the player and the "returning" feeling dies. `walkSpeed` is `2.5`, so the floor is `4.0`; the default `7` sits with a wide margin. This one is not left to memory: `EchoTuningProfile.OnValidate` rewrites `waveSpeed` up to the floor the moment a designer raises `walkSpeed` in the inspector, so the rule cannot be broken by a typo.
2. The fracture mask is computed once per ping and every arc of that ping reads the same mask. This is a readability decision, not an optimisation (read `Docs/DESIGN_DECISIONS.md`).
3. A ray that hits nothing means "there is no information", not "a distant surface". `d = +inf` and `amp = 0`, and the line breaks right there.
4. Balance is judged in pixels, not in world units. The sandbox camera is `orthographicSize = 6`, so on the short side of the screen 12 world units map onto 1080 pixels and each world unit is roughly 90 pixels. Any number in this table that ends up below 4 pixels, or below 25 pixels of shape change across the wave's lifetime, effectively does not exist in the game.
5. `crawlerPatrolSpeedMultiplier <= crawlerChaseSpeedMultiplier < 1` — even hunting, the Crawler is slower than the player's walk, and it never slows down when it hears you. `EchoTuningProfile.OnValidate` clamps the chase to `0.95` and the patrol to the chase; `MovementAndHearingTests` asserts both.

## The week 1 rebalance (date: 2026-10-17)

The player said in Play "the wave just arrives, nothing comes back from the objects". Measurement instead of guessing: with the GDD defaults (`gain0 = 0.28`, `baseThickness = 0.022`) the pillar's dent on the arc came to `0.119` world units — `10` pixels on screen, `2` pixels on the ribbon. The eye sees a circle there, not a pillar. Now:

| Parameter | Before | After | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gain0` | `0.28` | `1` | The dent reaches `38` pixels, so the arc actually reflects the object's own distance |
| `baseThickness` | `0.022` | `0.05` | The ribbon goes from `2` to `4.5` pixels; below this it breaks into dashes on a 1080p monitor |
| `maxRadius` | `9` | `6.5` | A ring larger than the room reads as a circle that came from nowhere; a shout's reach is `5.47` units, which covers the wall (`4.85`) and the box (`3.31` to `4.97`) |
| `minRadius` | `2.5` | `2` | A whisper should show something but not as much as a shout |

**Regression lock:** `Assets/Tests/EditMode/ArcReadabilityTests.cs` translates these same four claims into pixels (pillar dent ≥ 25 pixels, pillar vs the wall behind it ≥ 90 pixels apart in radius, the pillar centre as the innermost point of the arc, ribbon ≥ 4 pixels). If the tuning gets flattened again, this goes red — not the playtest.

**The same playtest, second complaint:** "sound only comes toward the player, nothing leaves him". A returning arc is inherently a ring collapsing from outside and shows nothing of the outbound part. For that same cause-and-effect feeling, `emissionPulseSeconds` opens a plain line in *the same cone* as the ping outward from the origin for 0.16 seconds. Deliberately shapeless: it contains no measurement, so the rule "only the returning wave carries information" is not broken. Its radius is always clamped below the returning carrier so the two rings cannot cross. **Lock:** `EmissionPulse_NeverOvertakesTheReturningCarrier` in `ArcMathTests.cs`.

## `_Hdr` and Bloom

The glow comes from two places: the `_Hdr` multiplier in the material `Assets/Art/Materials/Arc_Cyan.mat` (shader `Assets/Art/Shaders/EchoArc.shader`) and Bloom's `threshold` in `Assets/Settings/EchoVolumeProfile.asset`. For the cyan line to actually produce light you need:

```
arcColor(channel max) * _Hdr * shape(≈1)  >  bloom.threshold
```

With `arcColor ≈ 0.914` and `_Hdr = 2.5` the output is about `2.28`, which passes the threshold of `0.55`. If the line looks dark and unlit, do not raise `_Hdr` first: check the camera's Post Processing tick and the `Echo` layer first (see `Docs/PLAN.md`, "if the ring was not visible").

The warm creature tone is close to the same ceiling: `creatureArcColor` peaks at `1.0` on red, so a fully bright warm segment plus Bloom saturates toward cream rather than orange. It still reads as a different family from cyan in `Level02_Corridor` (verified on a frozen arc: warm and cyan vertices side by side in one mesh, distinct in the capture). If a future playtest says the two tones are hard to tell apart, lower `creatureArcColor` toward `#ff8a3d` before touching Bloom — the hue distance is the thing being tested, and Bloom's threshold is shared by both.

**And the glow only reaches a build if the Bloom override is really in the profile asset.** URP's build-time shader stripper scans every `t:VolumeProfile` asset under `Assets/`, and if none of them contains an *active* Bloom component it strips every variant of `Hidden/Universal Render Pipeline/Bloom` (`After scriptable stripping: 0` in the build log). The shader is then absent from the player, `isSupported` is false, and the renderer declines the pass — once per frame, in the log, never on screen. The editor cannot show you this failure: it compiles variants on demand and the session that created the profile still holds the live component in memory. `SceneKit.EnsureVolumeProfile` therefore writes the asset first and `AddObjectToAsset`s the component afterwards, and repairs a profile that has no Bloom instead of returning the first one it finds — the shape of the `objectReference: {fileID: 0}` trap in [TEAM_SETUP.md](TEAM_SETUP.md), in a place where the symptom was three systems away from the cause. **How to check a build in thirty seconds:** `grep -c Bloom Logs/player-build.log` should be non-zero and `After scriptable stripping:` under the Bloom passes should be `2`, and the shipped player's own `Player.log` should contain no `Bloom` line at all.

### How those numbers were taken (and one trap)

Through `Tools/Echo Maze/Echo Debug Window` in the editor: freeze the arc train so a frame can be studied instead of flown past, and read the pool's `d[i]` / `b[i]` / `broken[i]` plots. The same figures above were reproduced by re-running the fan on the live scene twice with identical results, which is what `PingRecord.seed` and the emitted-at frozen clock are for — determinism is the point, not convenience.

The trap, learned the expensive way: this week's only "bug" — a `NullReferenceException` from `Crawler.FixedUpdate`, where the seeded `pauseRandom` was gone while the serialized patrol data had survived — was not a game bug. **Enter Play Mode Options had drifted back on** on the lead's machine, against the setting week 1 turned off on purpose: with the reloads switched off, a script recompile restores serialized fields and leaves every non-`[SerializeField]` private field null. `ProjectSettings/EditorSettings.asset` is meant to sit at `m_EnterPlayModeOptionsEnabled: 0, m_EnterPlayModeOptions: 0`, and the general rule is that a measurement taken in a domain-reload-disabled editor is not a measurement of the game. What the four "drifts" actually were is in the test bridge, and it is deliberate: the MCP-for-Unity runner's `TestRunnerService.EnsurePlayModeRunsWithoutDomainReload` (`Library/PackageCache/com.coplaydev.unity-mcp@4384f8b518e8/Editor/Services/TestRunnerService.cs:520`) sets the flag to `true` with `DisableDomainReload` before **every** `TestMode.PlayMode` run — because the reload tears the bridge down and the run's result never reaches the caller — and restores it afterwards. Two consequences. (1) `git checkout --` on that file while the editor is running does not fix it: the value lives in memory and is written back over the revert on the next save, and since assigning `enterPlayModeOptions` re-enables the flag (our own comment at `SandboxSceneSetup.cs:35`), the two fields have to be written in that order and the file re-read afterwards to confirm. (2) **No PlayMode result obtained through the bridge is a measurement of the game** — every suite run this project has done through MCP executed with domain reload disabled, including the 32/32 recorded for the player art. It is a smoke test, and the numbers week 2 freezes have to come from the headless run with the editor closed, which `Docs/PLAN.md` already schedules for the close. The narrowing matters: the bridge's own restore puts the editor's *memory* back (verified 2026-10-28 — memory `False/None` while the file read `True/0`), so a session started by pressing Play is a real measurement and the brightness rows above stand; what goes stale is the file on disk, which is a hazard for whoever launches the editor next and for whoever commits `ProjectSettings/` without looking. `Validate Git Setup` proves the merge driver; nothing yet proves this file, and a check that does is worth five minutes in week 3 — a setting the project's own tooling flips on for every test run is the argument for it.

### Measured, still open

- **The metric is incidence-dependent, not only distance-dependent.** `NormalisedCurvature` divides by `d[i] · Δθ²`, which makes a flat plane measure exactly 1.0 *at normal incidence*; at grazing incidence the same plane measures `2/cos²ψ − 1`, i.e. 7 at 60° and 29 at 75°. In this 7 × 5 room it does not bite yet — every saturated sample in the `Wall_North` cone belongs to the `Pillar` or the `Crawler`, and none to a flat run — but looking *along* a wall rather than at it will eventually flare the wall. The fix is on record for week 3: store the hit normal's cosine per sample at emit time and divide by the plane's expected value, which costs one array and no extra raycasts.
- **Percentage-of-cone cannot express shape.** Everything above is a count; whether a human separates "wall" from "pillar" from "corner" by brightness alone is the readability session, and no number in this file answers it.

Four numbers were decided here rather than inherited:

- **Cell size `2` m.** One body (`0.25` m radius) fits with room to turn, and `interactionRange = 1` means standing in a doorway reaches the door while standing one cell back does not.
- **Camera `orthographicSize = 6`** — unchanged from the blockout — so the maze is two and a half screens tall and the wave's `6.5` m reach is a *local* reveal inside a much larger space. That is the whole reading of the level: the map is bigger than the echo, and the player carries their own flashlight of sound through it. `PlayerCameraRig` clamps to `42 × 30 / 2` minus half a cell, so the camera cannot show the void past the authored edge.
- **`168` wall cells become `40` colliders** (`MazeGrid.WallRects`, and the exact count is pinned by `MazeGridTests`). Not an optimisation: one collider per cell puts a distance jump on every cell boundary, `SurfaceRead` reads a jump as the edge of an object, and a long wall would break and brighten along its own length instead of reading as one flat sheet.
- **The door's cost.** `doorOpenLoudness 0.22` and `doorCloseLoudness 0.34` sit between the glass you walk on and the tap you read the room with, which is the ordering `NoiseStreamTests` now pins end to end. Opening is quieter than closing because closing is the move made while being chased. The **body** of a closed door is a `Default`-layer blocker (`1.8 × 1.8 m`), a layer outside the `960` echo mask; the wave still reads the two `0.8 m` echo panels and their `0.4 m` slit, which is narrower than the `0.5 m` body. See `ISSUES.md` #8 — the first layout left a `1.6 m` walk-through and the grid test could not see it.

**What is *not* tuned in this chapter:** there is no Crawler in the maze yet. The hearing radius `H = ρ·R(L)` and `ρ = 0.8` are still measured against the corridor, and a maze corridor is where a wrong `ρ` would actually show up — a creature that hears around corners makes the door a decision rather than a chore. That is a week-4 number, taken with a creature in the room, not guessed into a level that has none.

**The two 3/4 stills are not mirrors, and they are flipped anyway.** Each differs from its own mirror in **~25 % of the sampled pixels of its content box** — three times `Player_front`'s 8 % — because the artist keeps the rim light and the speculars on a fixed side instead of mirroring them with the body. So `flipX` on a diagonal is not a bit-exact reproduction of a delivered drawing; it is the same body lit from the opposite side. Accepted deliberately (the alternative was no diagonals this week), and recorded so the art team knows which two files to redraw rather than being handed a bug report about a boolean. Which half each file keeps **is** readable, but not from the silhouette — the body outline is symmetric to within `26` of `4096` px in both files, so the shape says nothing — and **from the light**: the cyan rim and eye mass sits `121` px **left** of the file's own centre in both 3/4 stills, against `+197` px in `Player_side`, whose facing is independently proven (the paragraph above). The light in this set sits on the side the character faces, so the profile is authored facing screen right and the two 3/4 stills are authored facing screen **left**, and the flip is the *disagreement* between those two facts rather than a property of either. `PlayerVisual` keeps them apart as `LooksLeft(pose)` and `IsAuthoredLeftward(file)`; the first cut had one list answering both, chose a convention instead of reading the art, and shipped the diagonals mirrored the wrong way round until the lead saw it in play. The four diagonal PlayMode cases assert the corrected pairing, and a file authored either way from now on is one entry in one list.

**A pose's facing and a file's authored facing are different questions, and the project has now paid for conflating them twice** (issue 5's non-mirroring export, issue 2's inverted diagonal flip). Both times the measurement that settled it was a centroid comparison, not a reading of the code.

The pose rule and the step phase are pure (`PlayerFacing`), which is what makes them testable at all: 14 EditMode cases cover the four sectors, every degree of the sweep, the ±45°/±135° edges, the `Atan2` wrap, the two helpers the sticky layer measures itself against, and the four `StepPhase` states; 19 PlayMode cases cover sprite selection, the mirror convention, the three stability gates above, the Shift flattening, the bounce being locked to `Player`'s own footstep clock, and the guarantee that a player with no `PlayerVisual` at all still runs a clean frame.

The motion was verified in `Level02_Corridor` in Play mode rather than only in the suite: walking right holds `localRotation.z = -0.0262` (the 3° lean) with `localScale = (1.010, 0.980)` (the landing dip), and the same frame with Shift held reads exactly `(0, 0, 0)` / identity / `(1, 1, 1)` while the pose still tracks the walk. The arcs draw over the body — `Visuals` is on `Default` (id 0) and `EchoField` on `Echo` (id 1).

**Open, and only a playtest closes it:** the dot was a flat teal disc at alpha 0.35; the character is a near-black body with a cyan rim and an eye at full brightness. The silhouette is the same size by construction, but the bright area is far smaller and the dark body is a different class of object from the faint cyan geometry the room is drawn with. "Is the player now too readable, or not readable enough?" is not answerable from these numbers.


## Wall memory and the footprint trail (2026-10-30)

Two own-mesh renderers (`WallMemoryView`, `FootprintTrailView`, built on `QuadMeshBuffer`) on the `Echo` layer at sorting orders `-10` and `-8`, under the live arcs (`0`). Neither touches `EchoRenderer.MaxQuads`. The memory rebuilds only when the store changed or a line is still fading in; the trail rebuilds each frame while it has dots.

**Is erasing every trail in about 4 s heavy?** No, by construction: at most `33` points, expiry is a pop from the tail, nothing allocates, and the draw is `<= 33` quads in one mesh. **Measured cost:** **0.0033 ms per frame** for 20 dots, measured 2026-10-30 by `TrailBufferTests.RebuildingAFullTrail_CostMeasured` (1000 rebuilds, EditMode) — about 0.02 % of a 16.7 ms frame. Estimate from the work done, not a measurement: 132 vertices written and one 33-quad mesh upload per frame.

**Decision recorded:** Shift-walking leaves footprints too (the owner did not ask for silent steps to be untraceable). The trail is the player's only; the Crawler leaves none. Prints within `0.4` m of the player are not drawn (the Echo layer sorts over the body).
