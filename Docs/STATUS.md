## The shape of the game today

```
Boot (menu) ──begin──▶ chapters page ──▶ I the maze ──▶ II the corridor ──▶ … ──▶ X the sump
    │                                                                              │
    └─ tune your voice                                                   victory panel ──▶ Boot
       (device picker)     every room is a text file; the scene that plays it is generated
```

A player can start the game from the menu, pick the microphone in-game, see the whole story as one rail on the chapters page, and walk ten rooms from the enemy-free maze down to the sump — every one of them ending on the victory panel, every one of them remembering its own best run. That is the milestone this stage asked for: **the shape is complete and the story has a middle**, not a mechanics playground with one room at its end.

`Sandbox` is the ray-fan lab, not the game. The other nine scenes in `Assets/Scenes` are chapters.
[Docs/LEVELS.md](LEVELS.md) is the level file: the ladder, the map legend, and the three edits that add a
chapter.

## M1 — what is actually built

### Flow and screens

- `Boot.unity` is the entry point: title, **begin**, **tune your voice**, **quit**, and a status line that
  prints the capture device the game really opened.
- `GameFlow` survives scene loads and owns the chapter list and the run's totals. The list is **ten
  chapters** and it is derived from `ChapterCatalog`, not written twice: finishing a chapter opens the next
  one, and finishing the last shows the victory panel with the run on it.
- `LevelSession` owns the chapter: pause (`Esc`), the checkpoint, the counters, and the exit hand-off.
- `RunController` owns one-hit death: black screen, "YOU WERE HEARD", retry under two seconds, back to the
  last sanctuary, with the level's authored state reset through its reset hooks.
- `HudView`, `PausePanel`, `VictoryPanel`, `MainMenu`, `CalibrationPanel`, `VoiceCalibration` and the
  microphone picker are generated by `UiKit`; re-running the bootstrap repairs them instead of stacking a
  second copy.
- The menu's **how to play** page is the field guide: seven cards — reading the dark, the waves you can
  make, moving and what it costs, what lives in the dark, the rest of the room, your voice, and the descent
  — stepped with two buttons or the arrow keys. The copy is `Assets/Scripts/Runtime/UI/GuidePages.cs` and
  says only what this build does: the crawler walks every chapter after the first, and the chapters page's
  own card explains the seal. `GuidePageTests` fails if a card outgrows the generated page;
  `BootSceneWiringTests` fails if the bootstrap stacks a second copy or leaves a label unwired.
  `-echo-guide-page <n>` photographs any card from a terminal.
- The victory panel prints the run's numbers: rooms, waves, times heard, and time, and plays the
  **victory emote** beside them when the escape lands (see below).

### The story page (2026-11-08)

- `Boot` has a **chapters** page drawn as a **rail**: one node per chapter down a single thread, and a
  dossier beside it reading out the selected room — its numeral, name, grade, what shares it, the sentence
  that carries the story into it, and the best run it has been walked in. A walked chapter's node is teal
  and stamped with its record, the room the player has earned is white and open, and everything past it is
  grey and **SEALED** until the room before it is finished. A sealed node can still be read: the story is
  the reason to keep playing. Click a node (or ↑/↓ and Enter) to select, Enter again to walk it.
- The rail's one animation is `ChapterRailPulse`: a pulse of light that walks down the thread to the node
  you chose, with cubic-out timing, and a ring that opens where it lands — the game's own wave-and-echo
  vocabulary used as a selection cue. At rest the selected node breathes very slowly (0.05 → 0.13 alpha over
  3.2 s of unscaled time), so the page is never completely still and never reads as loading.
- Choosing a node cross-fades the dossier instead of cutting it, so a page that changes under the hand says
  so. The dossier is the only thing that fades: the way out and the scoreboard stay put.
- The ten rooms themselves, the map legend, and the three-edit loop that adds a chapter are
  [LEVELS.md](LEVELS.md). Every grid chapter's scene is generated by `ChapterSceneSetup`; `ChapterLevelTests`
  proves each map parses, can be walked from spawn to exit, that its door gates that exit, and that nothing
  was authored into a pocket the player can never reach.
- Progress is `ChapterProgress`: one `PlayerPrefs` key per scene (`echo.story.<scene>.done/.best/.waves/.retries`),
  keyed by scene name so a room inserted in the middle of the story cannot inherit somebody else's progress.
  `GameFlow` owns the rule: chapter one is always open, a chapter opens when the one before it is walked, and
  every finished chapter records its own numbers — not the run's totals.
- The menu's first button says what it does: **begin** on a fresh profile, **continue · chapter II** after
  chapter one, **walk it again** once everything is walked. `GameFlow.ContinueIndex` answers where that is.
- Each level opens with one line of story, after the chapter title has faded: `ChapterOpening` reads
  `ChapterCatalog.OpeningForScene` and sends it through `LevelSession.Hint`, so the sentence uses the HUD's
  one notice label and there is no second text system.
- Copy for all of it is `Assets/Scripts/Runtime/Gameplay/ChapterStory.cs` — one entry per chapter plus the
  three strings of the sealed finale; a chapter added to the flow without an entry is caught by
  `BootSceneWiringTests`, and the page's own rules are `ChapterPageTests`.
- Verified 2026-11-08: EditMode **184 / 184** green — the suite grew rather than shrank: `ChapterLevelTests`
  now walks all ten maps, and the page's wiring test covers the rail. The rail was captured from the player in
  both states (`Screenshots/chapters_rail_sealed.png`, `chapters_rail_progress.png`), plus `menu_five.png`
  and `level_chapter_opening.png`. Captures come from `-echo-chapters`, a flag on the terminal hook; the sealed
  state is produced by removing the `echo.story.*` keys from the player's prefs.

### The level

- `Level01_Maze` is **a text file, not hand-placed YAML**: `Assets/Data/Levels/Level01_Maze.txt`, a 21 × 15
  grid at 2 m per cell. `MazeLevel` builds the colliders, the door, the sanctuary, the glass and the exit at
  runtime. The designer edits the file; the scene stays small and mergeable.
- The route has a spawn in the bottom-left, an exit in the top-right, one door in the middle band, one
  sanctuary before the door, a six-metre glass run on the spawn row, a round pillar signature and a bright
  box corner.
- The door is **one hinged leaf in the mouth of a straight corridor**, not a gate standing in the middle of a cell. The leaf's collider spans the full corridor width on the boundary between the corridor and the cell beyond it, flush with the two wall faces that flank the opening: closed, it is a wall across the corridor and the body cannot walk through it; open, the collider is switched off and the whole opening is clear. There are no jamb stubs, no `0.8 m` panels and no body-sized blocker — a stub beside a door is a little wall in a corridor, and that is exactly what it looked like in play.
- Since 2026-11-10 the door is also **drawn**: one amber leaf, hinged at one end, shown only when the player has earned it — a ping whose fan reached the leaf, or a body within `revealDistance` (`3.5 m`). Opening swings it 90° into the new cell over `0.4 s`; closing swings it back.
- **The level places only the interaction probe.** `Door` owns its leaf; `MazeLevel.BuildDoors` only reads the map for which way the corridor runs, which mouth the player reaches it from (the side reachable from spawn with the doors shut) and therefore which way the leaf swings. A door cell that is not a straight 1-cell passage with walls on both flanks stops the build with an error rather than producing a door on a bend — that is how Level 1's door ended up on the corner at `(11,7)`, and it now sits at `(10,7)`.
- The exit is a probe volume with `LevelExit`: stand beside it and press `E`. It is not drawn; the way out
  is found by sound, as the GDD asks.
- The wave is still the week-2 system: cone fan, returning arc train, curvature brightness, fracture mask,
  warm creature tint, touch marks, weak pulse, shimmer hook. **No enemy lives in the maze**, so the level is
  a pure tutorial, exactly as GDD 3.0 describes chapter one.

### Microphone

- `MicrophoneEchoSource` opens the real device, discards the first 0.25 s of uninitialised ring memory,
  reads one RMS window per frame and emits one ping per rising vocal event.
- `MicDeviceChoice` is the rule behind the in-game picker: `auto` skips every name containing `Monitor`,
  the list re-scans when a device comes or goes, and the saved name falls back to `auto` instead of
  failing to open.
- **Measured on this machine, in the player build, 2026-10-29:** the player logged
  `mic:Default Input Devi...` and `3 capture device(s): Default Input Device, Monitor of C-Media(R) Audio
  Digital Stereo (IEC958), C-Media(R) Audio Analog Stereo`. The menu screenshot shows the same line.
  The *gate opening on a human voice* is still a microphone-session question, not a code question; see
  [MIC_TEST_MATRIX.md](MIC_TEST_MATRIX.md) rows 3 and 6–7.

### The trace, and the lamp (2026-11-08)

- A touched wall is a **six-second trace** now, not a permanent line. The hold and the 1.2 s fade-out are
  `wallMemoryHoldSeconds` / `wallMemoryFadeOutSeconds`, the view rebuilds while any line is still aging, and a
  second touch on an existing line restarts its six seconds (`WallMemory.Add` stamps the merge, locked by
  `WallMemoryTests.ATouchAgain_RefreshesTheLineItMergesWith`).
- **Lanterns** are the game's first light that is not a sound, and the answer to "every screen is dark". The
  room places none of them: each chapter gives the player **two lamps, three in the hard and brutal rooms**
  (`LevelSceneKit.LampsFor`), and `Q` sets one down where the body is standing. A placed lamp is fixed — no
  carrying, no picking it back up — and while it stands it draws every wall within `lanternRevealRadius`
  (5.5 m) in warm light, faded towards the edge of its circle, for free: light makes no noise. The budget is
  `LanternStock` (pure, EditMode-tested), the spending is `LanternKit`, the drawing is `Lantern`, and the HUD
  prints `lamps n` under the cooldown sweep.
- The guide has a ninth card (`the lantern`), the chapter-one hints gained the lamp line, and chapter X's copy
  no longer claims its light is the only one in the game.
- Verified: `GameBootstrap.BuildEverything`, the EditMode suite (193 passed, 0 failed, including the new
  `LanternStockTests`), the PlayMode `LanternRevealTests`, and captures of a lit room and the new guide
  card from the player build.

### The palette, and the theme (2026-11-11)

- **Every event in the game now makes a sound, and the menu has a theme.** Fourteen events (interface,
  footsteps, glass, hints, the wave returning, doors, lamps, sanctuaries, the chase heartbeat) and three
  pieces of music (menu, a room's bed, the victory sting) are generated from code by `SoundSynth` and
  `MusicScore`, and played by `SoundBoard` — one board that survives scene loads and picks the cue from the
  scene that just loaded.
- The sound designer's palette replaces it file by file: `SceneKit.EnsureSoundBoard` wires any
  `Assets/Audio/<SoundId>.wav` into the board, so dropping a file in is the whole of the work. The three
  CC0 clips already in the project are unchanged.
- **The menu theme is a piece, not a pad** (rewritten 2026-11-11): eight bars in D minor at 60 BPM, a
  four-bar phrase answered by a four-bar phrase, the second landing on the tonic over the dominant so the
  loop point is a cadence. Pad and bass hold the harmony, a bell carries the tune, and the tune is played
  twice more behind it — a beat and a half late and an octave down, then three beats late and a whisper.
  The room answers the menu, which is what the game is about.
- **The menu was silent, and is not now.** `Boot` had no `AudioListener` in it — every level puts one on the
  player — so the theme and every button press in the menu were inaudible while the levels were fine.
  `SoundBoard.EnsureEars` keeps the board's own listener and gives it up to a player that has one; the two
  halves are pinned by `SoundBoardEarsTests`.
- **Measured:** the whole palette is **~1.9 s** of arithmetic in a cold process (~1.3 s warm), of which
  **43 ms** is the fourteen events (generated lazily on the main thread) and the rest is the three pieces of
  music (generated on a worker thread, clipped on the main thread when they land). The menu opens on its
  first frame; the theme fades in.
- Verified: the EditMode suite is **199 / 199** green and the new PlayMode ears tests are **2 / 2**
  (`TestResults.xml`, 2026-11-11), including seven palette tests — every event audible, no NaN or
  out-of-range sample, every loop joins without a step, the theme is long enough to be music, and the echo
  grows louder and falls in pitch with the shout.
- **Not verified:** batch mode has no audio device, so whether the palette *sounds* right is still a person
  with headphones. `Alert` and `GiveUp` are written and in the enum but not yet called: the Crawler's
  week-2 investigate stub has no transition to hang them on.

### The victory emote (2026-11-12)

- **Escaping the maze now plays an emote.** The reference art in `Victory-emote/` (seven separated layers
  plus `Final-Shot.jpeg`) is cut into six registered 1024 × 1024 sprites at editor-build time and
  composited in the victory panel: a white flash pops, the swirls open, the confetti falls, **VICTORY!**

- The run ends with `Time.timeScale = 0`, so `VictoryEmote` is driven from `Update` on unscaled time and

## What is real, and what is still a promise

These are deliberately listed so the next stage does not mistake a built room for a finished system:

- **`NoiseBoard` is not built yet.** Noise is still reported directly to the director; there is no single
  stream every listener subscribes to, and no `HeardBy` pure function yet. Walls, closed doors and noise
  zones do not attenuate hearing.
- **No suspicion meter and no `EnemyBrain`.** The Crawler in `Level02_Corridor` still uses the week-2
  proximity/hunt stub (`Investigate` = walk to the noise, `huntSeconds` deadline, then give up). It is not
  in the playable run as a chapter yet.
- **Hollows and wall memory are built.** `Hollow` is a safe spot (hearing × `hollowHearingMultiplier` =
  0.15 for quiet sounds, breaks above `hollowBreaksAbove` = 0.25 loudness, ignored by an enemy already
  chasing) with "You are hidden" / "You are exposed" HUD notices. `WallMemory` remembers every wall the
  player touched as a six-second neon trace, never re-read from colliders, so an opened door leaves a
  remembered wall where there is now an entrance. `Ctrl` / Listen mode and the marble are still not
  advertised on the menu.
- **No Listen mode.** `Ctrl` does nothing yet; warm enemy ripples and cool landmark ripples are M2.
- **No landmark audio, no exit hum.** The exit is found geometrically, not by its sound.
- **Chapter-one hints are built.** `LevelHints` feeds five one-shot lines (Space charge, F pulse, wall
  memory, Shift silence, E door) through `HintQueue` into the HUD notice label, once each per run. Only the
  maze turns them on; the Corridor leaves them off.
- **Death snarl.** `DeathSnarl` plays `Death_snarl.wav` flat at the moment of death, tracked by
  `DeathSnarlTests`.
- **Section reset** exists as hooks: doors reset via `Door.ResetToStart`, Crawlers are re-found and rewound
  at retry via `FindObjectsByType<Crawler>` (so no cached array reaches into destroyed objects). A full
  authored-state reset (marble count, noise-zone state, etc.) is still week 4 work.
- **Readability and `ρ`** are still measured on the corridor, not on the maze with a creature in it.

## Known open defects (phase 3, 2026-11-06)

Two items are knowingly left standing at the end of phase 3. Both are recorded here with the measurements
that got us to them, so the next pass starts from evidence rather than from a hunch.

### 1. The diorama's emissive wave does not appear in any capture

The corner glitch and the menu legibility are fixed and verified. The wave — the thing that was supposed to
make the diorama read as *echoing* — has never been seen to light the walls in a captured frame, in the
editor or in the player.

What has been ruled out, with the evidence:

- **Wiring.** `mazeWallsRoot` in `Boot.unity` points at the `Maze Walls` transform (fileID checked); the
  component is enabled and `walls.Length == 18` at runtime.
- **The shader and Bloom.** Setting `_Wave` on the *material* `Maze_Block.mat` makes all 18 walls blaze cyan
  (`Assets/Screenshots/material_wave.png`). So the shader reads the property, HDR values above 1 do reach
  Bloom, and the camera renders the geometry.
- **The per-renderer path.** Writing the same value through a `MaterialPropertyBlock` on those 18 renderers
  produced nothing (`manual_band.png`) and then only ~6 walls lit (`mpb_test.png`).

The first diagnosis was the SRP batcher: a property declared inside `CBUFFER_START(UnityPerMaterial)` can be
ignored by per-renderer overrides, so `_Wave` was moved outside the cbuffer in both passes of
`Assets/Art/Shaders/MazeBlock.shader`. **That change is in place but is not yet proven to have fixed
anything** — the manual paint test run after it still showed no lit walls.

The second diagnosis, which is the one to test first next time: **both manual-paint experiments were
invalid, because `MenuMazeEchoWave` was running and overwriting the block.** `OneWave()` writes every
renderer's `_Wave` every frame during a pulse and zeroes all of them when it settles, so a hand-painted band
is clobbered within one to two frames — which would look exactly like "nothing rendered", and exactly like
"only some walls lit" if the paint landed mid-band. The experiments proved the paint did not survive, not
that MPB does not work.

So the wave may be fine and only ever have been missed by the captures, or it may genuinely not render. The
two are separated by a measurement that does not involve painting by hand:

1. Enter play on `Assets/Scenes/Boot.unity`, and every ~0.4 s for 6 s read the live MPB back off the wall
   renderers (`GetPropertyBlock` → `GetVector("_Wave").w`) *and* take a screenshot in the same call. If the
   numbers sweep 0 → 1 → 0 on a ~4 s cycle while the frames stay dark, the wave logic runs and the render
   path is the bug. If the numbers stay flat at 0, the coroutine is not reaching the loop.
2. If it is a render-path bug, the next lever is the material's SRP-batcher status rather than the shader
   text — check whether `Maze_Block.mat` reports batched, and if in doubt give the walls their own material
   instances (`renderer.materials`) so the value cannot be batched away at all. 18 walls is not a
   performance conversation.
3. Capture timing is a separate trap: `-echo-shot-at` counts from player start while the wave clock starts
   at the menu's `Awake`, and the cycle is 2.6 s of dark followed by 1.6 s of band. A single early capture
   proves nothing; a burst across 8–13 s covers at least one full cycle.

### 2. The controls legend needed its own ground, not a bigger dim

The vignette wash is transparent in the centre by design, and the phase-3 dim column (`UI_dim.png`, α0.55)
covers the title and buttons — but the legend at y −360 sits below that ellipse, over the diorama's glowing
trim, and went unreadable. Two fixes, both generated: a 64×256 vertical fade (`UI_legend_band.png`) at black
α0.75 under the legend, and `UiKit.EnsureTextShadow` — a dark drop shadow (black α0.92 at 0,−2) on the label
itself, whose glyph colour went from `CyanFaint` α0.45 to full `Cyan`. The band alone was tried first and was
not enough: a translucent glyph lets the light through the letters, which nothing behind it can take back.

**Seen and confirmed** in `Screenshots/legend_final_crop.png` (and the full frame
`Screenshots/legend_final_3.png`): both WASD lines read clearly over the trim bar that passes straight
through them, at two different rotation angles with the corners clean and no wall past the frame. Neither
frame caught the wave mid-band, which is item 1 above and is the reason that item stays open rather than
being called a capture-timing miss.

## M2 — the next stage, in order

1. **One noise stream.** `NoiseBoard` + `HeardBy(distance, loudness, walls, closed doors, noise zones,
   hollows)` as a pure, heavily tested rule. Route the player's ping, footstep, bump, glass and door noises
   through it. The door and sanctuary already have seams to hang this on.
2. **Suspicion and the Crawler.** `EnemyBrain` with Dormant / Patrol / Curious / Investigate / Chase / Lost,
   seeded pauses, alert freeze and give-up cue. Remove the week-2 stub.
3. **Silence as a move.** Listen mode (`Ctrl`) with warm enemy ripples that vanish when an enemy stands
   still, and cool landmark ripples.
4. **A hiding place.** Hollow hearing multiplier and the "chase breaks cover" rule; sanctuary threshold
   behaviour for enemies.
5. **Memory trace.** Walls only, 10–15 s, suppressed in noise zones.
6. **Chapter two, properly.** The corridor gets its loop, hollow, doors and wake trigger, then joins
   `GameFlow.chapterScenes`; the victory panel then reports two rooms instead of one.
7. **Readability session** with three people who have never seen the game, on Linux, with headphones and a
   microphone. This is the go/no-go that the code cannot answer.

**Definition of done for M2:** a new player starts at `Boot`, picks their own input, finishes the maze,
then survives the corridor's first hunt by breaking line of hearing and hiding — and the run ends in the
victory panel with two rooms on it.

```
$P -echo-start-run -echo-pause -echo-shot-at 0.5 -echo-shot Screenshots/pause_fade.png -echo-quit
$P -echo-complete -echo-shot-at 0 -echo-shot Screenshots/victory_typing.png -echo-quit
$P -echo-complete -echo-complete-lead 2.2 -echo-shot Screenshots/victory.png -echo-quit
```

`-echo-start-run` takes the real menu path (so `GameFlow` is subscribed), `-echo-complete` ends the chapter,
`-echo-pause` and `-echo-calibrate` open the two panels no terminal click could otherwise reach, and
`-echo-quit` closes the player. Without `-echo-shot` the debug hook does nothing.
`-echo-shot-at <seconds>` moves the capture off its 2 s default, which matters now that the screens animate:
the entrance is still writing the menu under about 1.5 s, an emissive wave crosses the 3D diorama on a ~4 s
interval, the pause panel is mid-fade at half a second, and the victory summary is still typing at 0 s. A
capture meant to prove a finished layout should stay on the default; one meant to prove the motion should
name its time.

## How this file is maintained

- One row or one bullet per stage, dated, with the measurement that proves it.
- Move a claim from "promise" to "built" only when the build shows it, a test locks it, or a person saw it.
- The next stage is a list of **systems**, not a list of screens: a screen without its system is a promise.
- The GDD is the design, `PLAN.md` is the schedule, `TUNING.md` is the numbers, and this file is the state.
  If they disagree, the GDD wins and the other three get fixed in the same change.
